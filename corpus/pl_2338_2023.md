# Brazilian AI Bill — PL 2338/2023 (Curated Summary)

> **Authority level: LEGAL (bill under legislative process).** Curated summary
> for RAG grounding, prepared for the RAIA proof of concept, based on the text
> approved by the Federal Senate in December 2024. Not legal advice; the text
> may change during processing in the Chamber of Deputies. Verify against the
> official text at https://www.camara.leg.br / https://www25.senado.leg.br

## Purpose and Approach

PL 2338/2023 establishes general norms for the development, implementation,
and responsible use of artificial intelligence systems in Brazil. Like the
EU AI Act, it follows a risk-based approach, combined with a strong
rights-based foundation influenced by the LGPD (Lei Geral de Proteção de
Dados, Law 13.709/2018) and the Brazilian Constitution.

## Foundational Principles

The bill grounds AI development and use in, among others: the centrality of
the human person; respect for human rights and democratic values; free
development of personality; protection of the environment and sustainable
development; equality, non-discrimination, plurality, and repair of the
effects of discrimination; transparency, explainability, and auditability;
human review of decisions; reliability and robustness; and due process.

## Rights of Affected Persons

Persons affected by AI systems have the right to:

- Prior information about their interaction with an AI system.
- Explanation of decisions, recommendations, or predictions made by AI
  systems that produce relevant legal effects or significantly impact them.
- Contest AI decisions and obtain human review of decisions with relevant
  impact, considering the context and state of the art.
- Non-discrimination and correction of direct, indirect, illegal, or
  abusive discriminatory biases.
- Privacy and protection of personal data under the LGPD.

## Excessive Risk (Prohibited) Systems

The bill prohibits AI systems that:

- Employ subliminal techniques inducing behavior harmful or dangerous to
  health or safety.
- Exploit vulnerabilities of specific groups (age, social, physical, or
  mental condition) to induce harmful behavior.
- Are used by public authorities for social scoring that evaluates,
  classifies, or ranks natural persons based on social behavior or
  personality for access to goods, services, or public policies in an
  illegitimate or disproportionate manner.
- Enable the production or dissemination of child sexual abuse material.
- Assess criminal-recidivism risk based on personality traits or prior
  behavior analysis (autonomous crime prediction about individuals).
- Autonomous weapons systems without meaningful human control.
- Real-time remote biometric identification in publicly accessible spaces
  is restricted to narrow, legally authorized law-enforcement scenarios
  (e.g. flagrante delicto pursuit of serious crimes, search for missing
  persons), subject to judicial authorization.

## High-Risk Systems (Art. 17 area list)

AI systems are high-risk when used for purposes including:

1. Safety management and operation of critical infrastructure (traffic,
   water, electricity networks).
2. Education and vocational training: access, evaluation, and monitoring
   of students, including proctoring.
3. **Recruitment, screening, filtering, and evaluation of candidates;
   decisions on promotion or termination of contractual employment
   relationships; task allocation; and monitoring or evaluation of
   performance and behavior of workers.**
4. Evaluation of access to and maintenance of essential public and private
   services and benefits, including credit scoring and eligibility for
   public assistance.
5. Administration of justice, including uses that assist judicial
   authorities in investigating facts and applying the law.
6. Autonomous vehicles in public spaces.
7. Health applications, including diagnosis-support systems and medical
   devices.
8. Biometric identification and emotion-recognition systems.
9. Criminal investigation and public security (assessment of evidence
   reliability, predictive policing constraints).
10. Migration management and border control.
11. Content curation/recommendation at large scale where there is
    significant risk to freedom of expression or information integrity
    (as debated across versions of the text).

The competent authority may update the high-risk list based on criteria
such as scale, vulnerability of affected groups, potential for
discriminatory impact, and irreversibility of harm.

## Governance Obligations

**All AI agents (providers and deployers)** must establish governance
structures and internal processes ensuring transparency about system use,
data governance with bias-mitigation measures, legal bases for data
processing, and security throughout the lifecycle.

**High-risk systems additionally require:**

- **Algorithmic Impact Assessment (Avaliação de Impacto Algorítmico, AIA)**:
  documented assessment of risks to fundamental rights, benefits, mitigation
  measures, and residual risks, to be shared with the competent authority
  and kept updated. Public-sector deployments face additional publicity.
- Documentation and automatic logging (registro) of operations enabling
  auditability and traceability.
- Tests for reliability, robustness, and accuracy appropriate to intended use.
- Data governance measures to mitigate and prevent discriminatory biases,
  including representativeness analysis of training data.
- Technical explainability measures and information sufficient for human
  review of decisions.
- **Effective human supervision** of high-risk systems, enabling humans to
  understand, interpret, and intervene in the system's functioning.

## Civil Liability

The Senate-approved text ties liability of AI suppliers and deployers to
the Consumer Defense Code and Civil Code regimes, with the burden of proof
shifted in favor of affected persons in defined circumstances, and stricter
treatment for high-risk systems (regime details evolved across versions —
verify current text).

## Supervision and Sanctions

A national coordination arrangement (SIA — Sistema Nacional de Regulação e
Governança de Inteligência Artificial, coordinated by the ANPD in the
Senate text) supervises compliance. Sanctions include warnings, fines of up
to R$ 50 million or 2% of the group's Brazilian revenue per infraction,
publicization of the infraction, and partial or total prohibition of the
system, applying proportionality criteria.

## Practical Classification Heuristics for Teams

- Systems for recruitment/screening/promotion/termination are HIGH-RISK
  under the Art. 17 area list — this mirrors EU AI Act Annex III item 4,
  so classification analyses can usually be shared across jurisdictions.
- High-risk classification triggers the Algorithmic Impact Assessment, and
  the affected person's rights (explanation, contestation, human review)
  must be operationalized in the product itself, not only in documentation.
